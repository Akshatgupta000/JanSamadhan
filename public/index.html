Structured Innovation Methodology

From a Local Challenge to a Lasting Solution.

Unlike standard complaint portals that merely file grievances, JanSamadhan guides civic problems through a comprehensive intelligence, research, and deployment pipeline.

01 Citizen Input

Report

Citizens and field workers describe the issue with GPS coordinates, photos, severity, and impacted population.

02 Explainable AI

Understand

AI extracts entities, classifies domain taxonomies, and uses pgvector semantic embeddings to cluster duplicates.

03 Officer Review

Prioritize

A transparent 5-factor scoring model ranks urgency; government officers validate the challenge with recorded rationale.

04 Matching Engine

Collaborate

Algorithm identifies top university departments, labs, and faculty experts to formulate an interdisciplinary team.

05 Academic & CSR

Build

Student teams submit technical proposals, develop lab prototypes, and advance solutions to real community pilots.

06 Field Audited

Measure

Deployed solutions track sensor telemetry, water quality, or agricultural yield verified by district authorities.

Project Lifecycle: Solar Arsenic Filter

Current Stage: IRL 6 (Community Pilot) Team JalRakshak • BIT Mesra

This innovation was initiated following citizen reports from Khunti district. It progressed through sequential milestone stages approved by government technical reviewers.

Sequential Milestone Timeline

M1: Research & Baseline Validation (IRL 2) • Approved
Spectrophotometry testing confirmed arsenic contamination > 10 ppb across 8 test handpumps.
M2: Architectural Concept & BOM (IRL 3) • Approved
Continuous-flow bed design finalized with activated alumina and local terracotta pre-filters.
M3: Benchtop Lab Prototype (IRL 4) • Approved
Prototype demonstrated 98.4% arsenic removal in BIT Mesra water technology lab.
M4: Murhu Block Community Pilot (IRL 6) • Active Field Trial
Solar pump and GSM telemetry operational for 250 households. Field officer verification in progress.
